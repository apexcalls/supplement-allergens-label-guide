Supplement Labels and Allergens: What to Look For

For anyone with a food allergy or intolerance, a supplement label deserves a slower read than most people give it. Allergens in supplements are not always obvious: they can hide in flavourings, coatings, binders and botanical blends, and a product that looks plain can still carry milk, soy, gluten, egg, fish or tree-nut traces through its manufacturing process.

The first place to look is the allergen statement, usually printed near the ingredients list — phrases such as 'contains milk and soy' or 'manufactured in a facility that also processes tree nuts'. These statements are the manufacturer's own declaration, and they matter more than the marketing on the front of the bottle. But they are not the whole story: 'may contain' warnings are voluntary in many places, and a product without a warning is not automatically allergen-free.

The second place to look is the full ingredients list itself, including the 'other ingredients' section that many buyers skip. Capsule shells, coatings and anti-caking agents are all ingredients too, and they are where a hidden allergen most often sits. If an ingredient name is unfamiliar, look it up before you buy rather than assuming it is harmless — a few minutes of checking is cheap insurance.

A third habit is to check whether the product is independently tested and whether the brand publishes its allergen controls. Brands that take allergens seriously usually say so clearly: dedicated facilities, batch testing and plain-language allergen pages are good signs, while vague reassurance with no detail is not. Independent guidance helps here — The Supplement Rating publishes a label-checking guide that covers allergen statements alongside serving sizes and ingredient forms, and its reviews, such as a review of a propolis-based formula, quote the full label so you can check allergen details yourself before buying. When a label is complete and a brand is open about its controls, buying with an allergy becomes a decision you can make with confidence rather than a guess.
